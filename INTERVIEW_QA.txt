QUESTION 1: Why did you prioritize PR-AUC and Recall over Accuracy?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
In credit card default prediction, the classes are moderately imbalanced: 
~77.9% of borrowers repay on time, while only ~22.1% default.

If we build a completely naive "Dummy Classifier" that predicts non-default (0) 
for every single applicant:
- Accuracy = 77.88% (looks impressive on the surface).
- Recall = 0.0% (fails to detect a single defaulter).
- Defaulters missed = 100% of loans default without warning, resulting in massive 
  capital losses for Ignosis.

Accuracy gives equal weight to predicting the easy majority class. In lending, 
the financial penalty of a False Negative (approving someone who defaults) is 
orders of magnitude worse than a False Positive (rejecting someone who is good).

Therefore, we prioritize:
1. PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under the Curve): Isolates model discrimination 
   exclusively on the positive minority class (defaulters) across all operating 
   thresholds. It does not get artificially inflated by true negatives.
2. Recall / Sensitivity: Measures the fraction of actual defaulters caught by 
   the system: TP / (TP + FN).
3. ROC-AUC: Evaluates rank-order discrimination across the entire score distribution.


QUESTION 2: How did you prevent data leakage throughout the machine learning pipeline?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
Data leakage happens when information from outside the training dataset is used 
to create the model, leading to overly optimistic evaluation and poor production 
generalization. We prevented leakage through five strict engineering practices:

1. Target Isolation: The target column ("default_payment_next_month") was separated 
   from feature matrix X before any preprocessing or feature engineering.
2. Stratified Partitioning Before Transformations: We split data into 60% Train, 
   20% Validation, and 20% Test using stratified sampling to preserve the 22.1% 
   default rate in all sets, BEFORE computing any statistics.
3. Fit on Training Only: Scikit-learn transformers (StandardScaler, OneHotEncoder) 
   were fitted strictly on X_train. The validation, test, and live API payloads 
   are ONLY transformed using the frozen parameters (means, standard deviations, 
   and category encodings) learned on the training set.
4. Row-Wise Feature Engineering: All domain features (e.g. credit utilization ratio, 
   payment-to-bill ratio, maximum delinquency) are computed strictly on each applicant's 
   own historical records, never using cross-applicant aggregations or future months.
5. Preserved Final Test Partition: The test set was touched only once for the final 
   unbiased evaluation after all model selection and threshold tuning were completed 
   on the validation set.


QUESTION 3: What models did you compare, and why was Random Forest selected as Champion?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
We evaluated 5 diverse model families:
1. DummyClassifier (Baseline): Benchmark for class priors (PR-AUC: 0.2212, ROC-AUC: 0.5000).
2. Logistic Regression: Linear parametric model with balanced class weights (PR-AUC: 0.5141, ROC-AUC: 0.7661).
3. Decision Tree: Non-parametric tree with max_depth=5 and leaf regularization (PR-AUC: 0.5307, ROC-AUC: 0.7772).
4. Random Forest: Bagging ensemble of 150 decorrelated trees with max_depth=8 (PR-AUC: 0.5791, ROC-AUC: 0.7952).
5. XGBoost: Gradient boosted decision trees with scale_pos_weight=3.5 (PR-AUC: 0.5784, ROC-AUC: 0.7975).

Why Random Forest Won:
- Credit risk features contain heavy nonlinear interactions (e.g. high credit 
  utilization is safe if payment-to-bill ratio is 100%, but toxic if combined 
  with a payment delay). Linear models cannot capture this without extensive 
  manual interaction terms.
- A single Decision Tree captures interactions but easily overfits training noise.
- Random Forest averages 150 decorrelated trees, reducing variance by (1 - rho)/B 
  while preserving low bias.
- Random Forest achieved the highest PR-AUC (0.5791), superior probability 
  calibration (Brier score 0.1741), and required zero external C++ build dependencies 
  making it lightweight and reliable for production container deployment.


QUESTION 4: What domain features did you engineer, and why did they dominate SHAP importance?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
Raw financial data often conceals borrower behavior until transformed into ratios. 
We created 8 domain-specific financial features:

1. credit_utilization_ratio: Latest bill statement divided by credit limit 
   (BILL_AMT1 / LIMIT_BAL). Borrowers maxing out their credit lines (>80%) exhibit 
   liquidity distress.
2. max_delay_months: The maximum delinquency status across the past 6 months 
   max(PAY_1...PAY_6). A borrower who was previously 2+ months late carries residual risk.
3. months_delayed_count: Count of months where payment status was delayed (>0). 
   Measures habitual delinquency versus one-time oversight.
4. payment_to_bill_ratio: Total payments over 6 months divided by total bills. 
   Measures payoff capacity versus minimum payment treadmill.
5. pay_trend: Difference between latest delay and 6 months ago (PAY_1 - PAY_6). 
   Positive indicates deteriorating credit health; negative indicates recovery.
6. avg_bill_amt: 6-month average bill statement.
7. avg_pay_amt: 6-month average payment.
8. bill_to_limit_avg: Average bill to limit ratio.

Impact on SHAP:
In global SHAP rankings, "months_delayed_count" (#1) and "max_delay_months" (#2) 
outranked every raw feature in the dataset. In credit risk, behavioral delinquency 
patterns are vastly more predictive of insolvency than static credit lines or demographics.

QUESTION 5: Explain Logistic Regression mathematically (Sigmoid, Log-Odds, Loss, Odds Ratios).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
1. Sigmoid Function:
   Maps any linear combination z in (-infinity, +infinity) to probability p in (0, 1):
       sigma(z) = 1 / (1 + exp(-z))
   where z = beta_0 + sum_{j=1}^d (beta_j * x_j).

2. Log-Odds (Logit Transformation):
   Odds is the ratio of default probability to repayment probability:
       Odds = p / (1 - p)
   Taking the natural logarithm yields the logit, which is strictly linear:
       ln( p / (1 - p) ) = beta_0 + sum_{j=1}^d (beta_j * x_j)

3. Odds Ratio Interpretation:
   If we increase a standardized feature x_j by 1 unit while keeping all other 
   features constant:
       New_Odds = Old_Odds * exp(beta_j)
   - If beta_j > 0: exp(beta_j) > 1 -> Feature increases risk of default.
   - If beta_j < 0: exp(beta_j) < 1 -> Feature decreases risk of default (protective).

4. Binary Cross-Entropy Loss with L2 Regularization:
   Parameters beta are learned by minimizing negative log-likelihood (log loss):
       L(beta) = - (1/N) * sum_{i=1}^N [ y_i * ln(p_i) + (1 - y_i) * ln(1 - p_i) ] + (1 / 2C) * ||beta||_2^2
   The penalty (1 / 2C) prevents weights from blowing up due to collinearity between 
   consecutive monthly bill amounts.

5. Class Weighting in Loss:
   To prevent 78% non-defaulters from drowning the gradients, balanced weighting 
   multiplies the positive class loss by w_1 = N / (2 * N_1) approx 2.26, forcing the 
   optimizer to penalize errors on defaulters more heavily.


QUESTION 6: Explain Tree Models, Overfitting, and Random Forest Variance Reduction.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
1. Split Criterion (Gini Impurity):
   Measures how often a randomly chosen element from a node would be incorrectly labeled:
       G = 1 - sum_{k=0}^1 (p_k)^2 = 2 * p_0 * p_1
   At each split, the tree maximizes Impurity Reduction:
       Delta G = G_parent - (N_left / N_parent)*G_left - (N_right / N_parent)*G_right

2. Why Single Trees Overfit:
   An unconstrained tree splits repeatedly until leaves are pure (Gini = 0). It captures 
   idiosyncratic sample noise, resulting in low training error (high bias-free) but 
   extreme test variance.
   Regularization: Controlled via max_depth, min_samples_split, and min_samples_leaf.

3. Random Forest Theorem (Variance Reduction):
   Consider an ensemble of B trees, each with individual variance sigma^2 and average 
   pairwise correlation rho between any two trees:
       Var(Ensemble) = rho * sigma^2 + ( (1 - rho) / B ) * sigma^2
   
   As B (number of trees) grows large (e.g. 150), the second term approaches zero:
       Var(Ensemble) -> rho * sigma^2
   
   To reduce total variance further, we must minimize correlation rho between trees.
   Random Forest does this via two mechanisms:
   - Bootstrap Aggregation (Bagging): Each tree is trained on a different bootstrap sample.
   - Random Subspace Feature Sampling: At each node split, only m = sqrt(d) features are 
     evaluated. This prevents dominant features (like PAY_1) from appearing at the root 
     of every tree, decorrelating the trees and drastically lowering rho.


QUESTION 7: What is probability calibration and why is it critical in FinTech?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
Discrimination (ROC-AUC) only tells us if defaulters score higher than non-defaulters; 
it does NOT mean the score is an accurate, real-world probability.

A model is calibrated if:
Among a group of applicants assigned a 30% default probability, exactly 30% actually default.

Why this is critical for Ignosis:
1. Expected Loss Calculation:
   In banking (Basel II/III frameworks), loan reserves and provisions depend on:
       Expected Loss (EL) = Probability of Default (PD) * Loss Given Default (LGD) * Exposure at Default (EAD)
   If PD is uncalibrated (e.g. compressed between 0.35 and 0.55), the Expected Loss 
   calculation is distorted, leading to either reckless under-capitalization or lost business.
2. Risk-Based Loan Pricing:
   Interest rates are priced as: Base Rate + Cost of Capital + Expected Loss Margin. 
   Uncalibrated probabilities prevent accurate risk-adjusted pricing.
3. Metric:
   We evaluate calibration via Brier Score: (1/N) * sum (p_i - y_i)^2 (our Random Forest 
   achieved 0.1741, close to the theoretical minimum for this distribution) and Reliability Diagrams.


QUESTION 8: What do SHAP values mean, and why must they NOT be interpreted as causal proof?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is based on cooperative game theory. It calculates 
the marginal contribution of each feature across all possible subsets of features:
    phi_j = sum_{S subset F \ {j}} [ |S|! (|F| - |S| - 1)! / |F|! ] * [ f(S union {j}) - f(S) ]

Key Properties:
- Local Accuracy: sum(phi_j) = f(x) - E[f(x)] (The sum of SHAP attributions exactly equals 
  the difference between the applicant's prediction and the base population risk).
- Consistency: If a model changes so a feature contributes more, its SHAP value cannot decrease.

Why SHAP is NOT Causality:
- SHAP explains internal model behavior based on statistical correlations in training data.
- It does NOT prove causal counterfactuals in the real world.
- Example: Having a lower credit limit (LIMIT_BAL) may have a positive SHAP value for default risk. 
  This does NOT mean lowering an applicant's limit caused them to default. Rather, cautious underwriters 
  in 2005 had historically assigned lower limits to borrowers exhibiting risky behavior.
- Confusing correlation with causality in lending can lead to discriminatory or counter-productive policies.

QUESTION 9: Why did you choose a decision threshold of 0.38 instead of the standard 0.50?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
In academic ML, 0.50 is used arbitrarily because it assumes symmetric error costs. 
In commercial FinTech lending, error costs are highly asymmetric:
- Cost of False Negative (Default Loss): If an underwriter approves someone who defaults, 
  Ignosis loses the principal balance minus any recovery, plus collection overhead (~NT$ 50,000).
- Cost of False Positive (Opportunity Loss): If we reject a creditworthy applicant, we only 
  lose the net interest margin and marketing acquisition cost (~NT$ 8,000).

The cost ratio is ~6.25 to 1. Missing a default is 6.25x more expensive than rejecting a good applicant.

By sweeping thresholds t from 0.05 to 0.95:
    Total Cost(t) = (FN(t) * 50,000) + (FP(t) * 8,000)

The total cost curve reached its global minimum at threshold t* = 0.38.

Impact on Held-Out Test Set (5,989 applicants):
- At threshold 0.50: Recall was 61.25%, missing 513 defaults (Total Cost: NT$ 31.44M).
- At threshold 0.38: Recall jumped to 77.95%, catching 221 additional defaulters and 
  leaving only 292 missed defaults (Total Cost: NT$ 27.65M).
- Net Business Value: 12.04% financial loss reduction, saving NT$ 3,784,000 in bad debt.

QUESTION 10: How does your FastAPI service guarantee that production predictions use 
identical preprocessing as training?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
1. Unified Pipeline Serialization:
   We bundle the entire transformation logic (CreditRiskFeatureEngineer + ColumnTransformer) 
   and the final classifier inside a single Scikit-learn Pipeline object serialized as 
   best_model.joblib. We do NOT maintain separate standalone scaler or encoder files.
2. Strict Pydantic V2 Schemas:
   The API validates incoming JSON payloads using the ApplicantFeatures schema:
   - Field data types (float, int).
   - Domain bounds (LIMIT_BAL > 0, 18 <= AGE <= 100, PAY_i in [-2, 8]).
   - Rejects invalid requests with HTTP 422 Unprocessable Entity before reaching the model.
3. Exact Column Alignment:
   The CreditRiskService extracts the applicant payload into a Pandas DataFrame using 
   ALL_RAW_FEATURES, ensuring identical column names, ordering, and data types as training.
4. Deterministic Transformation:
   Calling pipeline.predict_proba(df) applies the frozen training scalers, category encodings, 
   and feature engineering formulas, completely eliminating training-serving skew.

QUESTION 12: If this system was deployed at Ignosis, how would you handle fair lending regulations?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
1. Regulatory Context (ECOA & FCRA):
   Under the US Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) Regulation B, creditors cannot 
   discriminate based on protected attributes (race, color, religion, national origin, 
   sex, marital status, or age).
2. Mitigation Strategy for Ignosis:
   - Feature Removal: In production, protected attributes (SEX, MARRIAGE) should be 
     removed from the candidate feature list.
   - Disparate Impact Auditing (The 80% / Four-Fifths Rule):
     We must calculate the Selection Rate (Approval Rate) across demographic cohorts:
         Adverse Impact Ratio = Selection Rate (Protected Group) / Selection Rate (Reference Group)
     If this ratio is less than 0.80, the model exhibits disparate impact and cannot be deployed 
     without remediation.
   - Explainable Adverse Action Notices:
     Under FCRA, if an applicant is denied credit, the lender must provide specific key reasons. 
     Our local SHAP attribution module (explain_single_applicant) provides the exact top 
     financial factors (e.g. recent 2-month delay on card, credit utilization > 90%) to generate 
     legally compliant adverse action letters.
   - Human-in-the-Loop:
     The system is strictly designed as a decision-support prototype. Borderline or high-risk 
     cases are routed to human senior underwriters for comprehensive manual review.


QUESTION 13: How would you monitor this model in production for drift?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
We monitor three forms of drift:
1. Data Drift (Covariate Shift):
   Input feature distributions change over time (e.g. macroeconomic inflation causes 
   higher credit card balances). We compute the Population Stability Index (PSI) weekly:
       PSI = sum ( (Actual% - Expected%) * ln(Actual% / Expected%) )
   - PSI < 0.10: Stable distribution, no action.
   - 0.10 <= PSI < 0.25: Moderate drift, investigate features.
   - PSI >= 0.25: Significant drift, triggers automated retrain alerts.
2. Concept Drift:
   The relationship between features and default changes (e.g. an economic recession 
   causes borrowers with clean credit histories to default). We monitor rolling 30-day 
   and 90-day delinquency outcomes as loans mature and track degradation in PR-AUC and ROC-AUC.
3. System Telemetry:
   Track p95 inference latency, requests per second (RPS), and HTTP 422/500 error rates 
   via Prometheus and Grafana dashboards.
